# Signal relationship evaluation protocol

## Scope and ground truth

This protocol evaluates the **Python prototype's relation output**, not identity, security, abuse prevention, or the C confidence score. It must never convert a relation into authentication. Define the deployment, policy, signal availability, population and intended use before selecting samples. Include independent devices on shared NAT, distinct cloned VMs, OS reinstall, lost collector permissions, hardware replacement and attacker-controlled signal rotation or forgery. Record independent units and how labels are established; avoid treating repeated observations from the same device as independent participants. Separate denied, missing-required and inconclusive outcomes from below-threshold relations; do not silently count them as true negatives.

For a future field study, obtain informed consent and a reviewed data-minimization/retention plan; define inclusion, recruitment, ground truth, dates, collection permissions, opt-out/deletion, and analysis in advance. Report stratum sample counts, selection bias, missing data, correlated observations, confidence intervals appropriate to sampling clusters, and how much the collected sample differs from the target deployment. Never publish raw identifiers or pair-level linkable data. Separate an adversarial bypass exercise from a naturalistic sample. Synthetic values alone cannot validate what real devices disclose or how often genuine users experience change.

## Reproducible synthetic model

Run `python3 tools/synthetic_stability_evaluation.py --samples 200 --seed 4301` and `python3 tests/test_stability_evaluation.py`. [Recorded output](../Test-Results/unreleased/synthetic-stability-evaluation.json) contains 200 isolated pairs in each of eight explicitly defined scenarios (1,600 pairs). Each starts a fresh memory engine, with the standard preset, threshold two including IP, high enough quota for both requests, and three supplied synthetic signals. A second sample compares selected cases with SQLite. Only aggregate results are emitted. The seed and exact transformation rules and assumed probabilities are in the script, not estimated from a real population.

The scenario labels are **assumptions**: stable, reinstall, permission loss, hardware replacement and total signal/IP rotation are treated as same-device pairs; NAT neighbors, distinct VM clones and a forged replay are different-device pairs. For example, the NAT model assumes a 10% chance of a shared board string and cloned VMs share machine and product values. Reinstall changes machine ID and sometimes product UUID; hardware replacement changes board serial and sometimes product UUID. Permission loss randomly omits required and optional values. The full-rotation case assumes all three signals and IP change; forged replay assumes attacker access to the three submitted values. These probabilities are illustrative, not estimates or claims of representativeness, and attacker access is not established by the experiment.

For seed 4301, shared-NAT pairs produced 25/200 related under the synthetic model; clones and forged replays produced 200/200 related. Full rotations yielded 200/200 below threshold. Reinstall yielded 15/200 and hardware replacement 55/200 below threshold; permission loss yielded 60/200 invalid, 42/200 below threshold and 98/200 related. These counts are not a real false-positive rate, user count, native stability measurement or evidence of a production bypass rate. Wilson intervals shown in the machine-readable report quantify binomial sampling variation **conditional on this model's independent draws only**. They exclude uncertainty from the assumptions, prevalence, data quality, recruitment, correlation and real deployment; deterministic scenario definitions are not population sampling. Invalid decisions are not classified as below-threshold. The simulation exercises the policy engine, not actual collectors or real consented subjects.

A representative empirical deployment claim requires the separately designed and reviewed study above. This protocol deliberately makes no such claim and does not imply that signals are authentic or robust to forgery, cloning, rotation, shared networks or host compromise.
